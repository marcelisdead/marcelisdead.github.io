Hello! I am Marcel Bornstein, artist, sculptor, and XR game developer from Queens, NYC. My work uses mundane objects, rules, and rituals in an absurd exploration of what it means to be human.

I've created with stone, concrete, and steel, but most of my early sculpture used found objects and played with the ideas of them. Much of my work is ephemeral, so I use images and video to present my sculptures, games, and theatrical experiences.

I often begin with an absurd premise that can develop through years of investigation into science, technology, history, human culture, and behavior.

In thinking about humanity's objects and our experiences with them, I came to realize that we would soon step outside the limits of physical reality. That we and our objects would exist in both digital and physical spaces, and the ideas of objects could exist in space with us. Not to mention that we could journey into the space of ideas ourselves.

This led me to learn everything I could about creating virtual objects, spaces, and experiences. Since 2013, I've been developing XR games and experiences that invite people into these worlds of strange ideas.

I design and develop my games in Unity, building systems for human interaction, creating 3D models, and developing visual effects tools. Sound is one of the biggest ways that we understand the world, so I consider it a core dimension of my work.

In Wellsoon, a creepy collaborative virtual layer on the planet, people collect color to draw on walls in real-world streets, inside a drug commercial about evolution.

In Server Update, a superpowered video game character escapes into the world inside a 90s computer during an update, exploring personal and societal trauma caused by shifts in technology and power.

In my live gameshow Pizza Party, I film and host through a VR headset, guiding players through a fever dream game of life while Death plays doom metal just off camera. Bathed in red light at an office birthday near hell, players respond to prompts ranging from drinking a glass of water to sharing their experience of love.

In my next project, Third Party, players will travel through American history, making decisions to survive within systems that their own actions may help sustain in a surreal multiplayer VR experience of history and ritual.

I hope people find my work fun and through it discover new ways of seeing the world.
